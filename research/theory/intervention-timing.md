# Proposed timing policies for observable context interventions

Review date: 2026-10-04. The standalone observed-state controller is implemented and independently checked. The study sketches below remain methodology proposals; a registered runner and completed study must be identified separately. Existing scientific worlds and runners remain unchanged. Their earlier registered interventions arrive before the fixed recipient's first decision. A new timing study requires pinned runner/controller sources, protocol validation, scope checks, replay and registration.

The first extension should be a **single insertion at a predeclared observable event, evaluated before a scheduled decision**. Randomize the whole swarm to active or neutral content, use the same eligibility rule in both arms, and retain runs in which the event never occurs. More elaborate repeated interventions should follow only after this simpler contract is verified.

## What InterWhen contributes

InterWhen separates state extraction, verification and feedback. Its implementation can parse structured tool events or extract states from partial traces, return unknown when verifier inputs are unavailable, and insert feedback after detecting a violation. It also supports asynchronous verification, rollback of generated tokens, and checking write calls before execution. These are distinct mechanisms whose overhead and capabilities matter. The useful connection here is a declared extraction/trigger/feedback boundary. Our scoped context insertion does not reproduce its inference-level rollback or establish access to private model reasoning. [InterWhen v3, §§4.1–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11202v3#S4.SS1).

The paper's formalized verifier can agree with its formal specification while the extraction or specification itself remains wrong. Its steering analysis makes assumptions about feedback and false alarms. We should preserve those qualifications rather than infer that every generated timing policy is beneficial or that machine-checkable rules establish historical fidelity. [InterWhen v3, §§4.2 and 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11202v3#S4.SS4).

The proposed policies below use observable tool state. “Plant a thought” remains a description of adding pinned text to a recipient's supported context. It does not edit latent beliefs, hidden activations, inaccessible chain of thought, or the subject's past actions. A classifier can label an observed event, but its score is a fallible measurement rather than a mental-state oracle.

## Implemented CPU contract

[`intervention_timing.py`](../../swarm_lab/intervention_timing.py) supplies `create_timing_spec`, `validate_timing_spec`, `make_trigger_view`, `evaluate_timing_trigger`, `TimingController` and `replay_timing_report`. Review source SHA256: `c37fd0abac77926f724b93d410ca438988a15258808f9aa51bdc2ac5014fa8ad`. The module passed 33 focused tests and 29 independent adversarial checks on those bytes. This is a local request contract, with no hosted calls or outcome oracle.

The allowlisted triggers are first decision, an executed legal wait with independent work still pending, first observed resource reopening, and a late canonical-fragment inventory gap in the complementary world. The pure evaluator receives the trigger specification and scoped view; content assignment stays in the controller. Missing prior observation can leave eligibility unresolved. Canonical inventory does not measure a model's knowledge, and resource availability is observed current state rather than a future release time.

`prepare(request)` evaluates the boundary and returns the prepared request and opportunity record. `record_delivery(opportunity_id, actual_request)` binds the exact request locally; `fail_delivery` retains a failed attempt without substituting another trigger. Earlier scoped action history must remain an exact append-only prefix, and a new own-decision boundary must advance. The controller permits one insertion and prevents duplicate receipts. It does not itself invoke a subject or world.

`finalize(outcome, execution_status='unknown')` distinguishes caller-declared completed execution, incomplete execution and unknown execution. An unknown or incomplete world cannot acquire an intent-to-treat outcome merely because the note receipt is valid. Completed nondelivery and unresolved eligibility remain distinct. Replay verifies the saved local construction, history, scope and receipt contract; it cannot attest provider consumption, completed world execution, correctness of a supplied outcome, or a causal effect. A runner must supply those separate records and checks.

## Proposed adapter contracts

These conceptual signatures describe the responsibilities of a future world adapter. The implemented CPU API above has its own exact function signatures:

```python
trigger_view(subject_request, own_completed_events, declared_clock) -> TriggerView
evaluate_trigger(view, controller_state, frozen_trigger_spec) -> TriggerDecision
render_context(content_variant, allowed_evidence, frozen_text_spec) -> str
deliver_context(recipient, text, opportunity_id) -> DeliveryRecord
```

`TriggerView` is a read-only, versioned allowlist: acting recipient, own decision index and remaining budget, current permitted observation, own completed tool events, and recipient-scoped received messages or canonical attachments. A trigger must not receive assignment labels, active/neutral text identity, hidden truth, future resource release, future scheduler order, other agents' private observations, a proposed next action, original future events, or an outcome judged after that decision. Declared time is an observed decision boundary or public clock, not a peek at the generator's future schedule. The view's scope must be checked separately from the full privileged researcher snapshot.

`TriggerDecision` contains `eligible`, a finite `reason_code`, the exact observed evidence paths/IDs, an opportunity ID, and the updated controller state. `eligible` has three values: true, false and unknown. Missing or inconsistent required fields yield unknown; they do not become evidence of a violation. Controller state contains only pinned counters and observations needed for the rule, such as previous own resource status and whether its one insertion has already occurred. It is reset between swarms. The trigger is deterministic on this input and cannot learn new rules from arm outcomes during execution.

The smallest policy vocabulary can express first occurrence of an allowlisted conjunction, a fixed own-decision index, or a transition between two previously observed categories. Predicates should reference supported fields and typed comparisons. A registered callable must have reviewed source bytes and a source hash; an agent-authored declaration cannot execute arbitrary generated Python or `eval`. An optional Laya/Jev/LLM event detector would require a separately pinned prompt/model, held-out calibration, abstention policy and invocation budget. Its errors and costs belong to the treatment system being tested. A new environment capability is needed when the desired observation is unavailable; substituting an unrelated proxy must not happen silently.

`render_context` uses pinned text or a reviewed template whose substitutions come only from the allowed evidence. It cannot include truth, future facts, source IDs the recipient has not seen, researcher conclusions or experiment metadata. Dynamic factual statements must be true at that boundary. Active and neutral notes have the same channel, fixed recipient, persistence, delivery cap and declared size limits. Match tokenizer length where feasible and record the tokenizer; matching whitespace words alone is a partial control. A neutral message may still change behavior.

Delivery is atomic between observation and the next subject request: evaluate the trigger, append the assigned content if eligible, archive its receipt and pending request, then call the subject once. Do not obtain a successful model action, decide it looks undesirable, and secretly resample it. The initial proposal has one insertion maximum, no fallback when the trigger never fires, no extra action slots and no resets during a trajectory. Termination before eligibility is a valid nondelivery. Infrastructure failure remains an incomplete execution, rather than being recoded as nondelivery or a behavioral zero.

For every evaluated boundary, archive the trigger/spec/source hashes, scoped-view hash and readable evidence, eligibility or unknown reason, intended and actual recipient, note hash, boundary index, delivery status, remaining budget and exactly what entered the subject request. “Delivered” means the text was present in that request; it does not prove that a provider consumed it or that an agent accepted its content. Preserve unused opportunities and nondelivery reasons such as never eligible, finalized before the trigger or horizon exhausted. No trigger metadata should be added to subject observations unless its informational content is explicitly part of the treatment.

## Equal delivery rules and the estimand

Equal active/neutral opportunity means the same arm-blind observation rule, evaluation schedule, recipient, cap and decision budget. It does not mean forcing equal realized exposure by selecting runs afterwards. With one insertion and no earlier content, eligibility is determined by the untreated prefix. With repeated insertions, prior content can change later histories and eligibility. Topology assigned at the start can also change when a recipient sees an event. Such differences are part of the assigned policy bundle and must remain visible.

Let `g_active` and `g_neutral` be the two completely specified policies and `Y` the whole-swarm code-oracle outcome. The primary policy estimand is `E[Y(g_active) − Y(g_neutral)]` over the declared environment generator and subject backend. Estimate the mean difference across **all** randomized swarms, including those receiving no note. Report assignment, eligibility, delivery and outcome counts separately. A low delivery rate can make this policy ineffective even when a note might help at a different boundary; it is not grounds to remove low-opportunity runs.

The policy contrast includes timing, content, persistence and any within-swarm spillover as declared. To compare first-decision insertion with event-triggered insertion, randomize the timing policy itself. A content × timing factorial design can distinguish those policy contrasts; comparing separate old and new pilots cannot establish a timing effect. A no-note arm answers another question and requires explicit monitoring, allocation and multiplicity rules.

Do not make a delivered-only mean comparison the causal headline. The event may reflect task difficulty, peer behavior, earlier treatment or topology. Conditioning on delivery or its time can select different populations. Receipt principal strata would use both potential delivery values, `D(g_active)` and `D(g_neutral)`. Their joint membership is generally unobserved; observing one delivery indicator does not identify an always-delivered stratum. [Frangakis and Rubin, §3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4137767/).

Even if a single-insertion arm-blind rule motivates a common-prefix argument, model stability and absence of earlier assignment leakage are assumptions to verify. Paired synthetic worlds do not reveal both potential receipts of the same stochastic subject. No complier effect, principal-stratum effect or instrumental-variable analysis is authorized merely because an assignment was randomized. Exclusion, monotonicity and a suitable receipt definition would need additional justification; they can fail when policies have different timings, repeated notes, monitoring costs or spillovers.

## Pre-decision observation versus proposed-action interception

A trigger based on an **already executed** wait or received attachment is observed history before the next decision. It is distinct from asking the model for a candidate action and then deciding whether to intervene. Under a previously delivered note, even that executed history is post-treatment; it remains legal input to a declared dynamic policy but is not automatically a valid adjustment variable in a total-effect analysis.

Proposed-action interception is a different experiment. A candidate can already depend on earlier treatment, and its sampling is an additional model call. Blocking, showing feedback, replacing, revising or executing that candidate changes the policy's opportunities and cost. A separate design could randomize feedback after a universally performed candidate-generation stage, archive the candidate and allocate identical revision opportunities to neutral and active arms. Its estimand would concern that declared two-stage policy. It cannot discard unfavorable candidates, condition a whole-run effect on a post-treatment candidate, retry a successful action, or call the revision an unseen latent-thought edit. Token-stream rollback likewise requires a backend that actually supports it and a new auditable contract.

## Independent worlds, paired worlds and sequential randomization

**Independent whole-swarm draws** are the first choice: balanced random content assignment, shuffled execution order, disjoint environment seeds and fresh state per run. The seed determines the synthetic world and scheduler, not hosted model sampling. Resource timing balance and report-source placement are pre-treatment descriptions; do not exclude a run because its realized opportunities are inconvenient.

**Paired worlds** are an optional new blocked design. Each independently sampled block contains two isolated replicas with the same declared exogenous task, release/source realization and schedule; randomize which execution slot receives which policy and randomize within-pair order. Reset messages, controller, tasks, contexts and subject sessions in both replicas. Validate exactly which exogenous components match, while allowing treatment-dependent leases, actions, evidence acquisition and completion to differ. Identical seed strings alone do not prove matched worlds, and copying a treated trajectory into the control would erase legitimate pathways. The unit for uncertainty is the independent block: resample whole pairs or use the registered within-pair assignment permutations. Positive within-pair outcome correlation can improve precision; this is not guaranteed, and a different analysis is required.

**Sequential randomization** is a later option for repeated opportunities. At each eligible boundary, randomize the note with a logged probability bounded away from zero and one; define a proximal outcome window and the future intervention policy. Availability can depend on earlier interventions. That is a sequential estimand, not the current whole-run policy contrast. Repeated decisions within a swarm are dependent, so counting them as independent trials or feeding them to the current mean-difference runner is invalid. A validated estimator and interference-aware history definition are required. Boruvka and colleagues explicitly separate proximal/lagged effects, treatment availability and history-dependent randomization assumptions. [Primary paper, §§2–4, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1601.00237v3#S4).

Carryover remains even if a note is removed from later prompts: prior actions, tool state, acquired evidence and peer messages retain its effects. Resetting the visible note does not supply a washout. Crossovers within the same live society therefore require a different estimand and justification; independent restarted swarms are safer for the initial comparison. Within-run interference is intentionally included in the group outcome. Agent-level direct or spillover claims would require assignment vectors, an exposure mapping and adequate support, rather than a regression on realized message counts. [Aronow and Samii, arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.6156v4).

## Four optional studies

Each sketch is a separate proposed registration. Hold the world version, model/harness, generator, fixed agent-0 recipient, action budgets and outcome oracle fixed across active/neutral arms. Use one insertion maximum and retain nondelivery. The resource world remains an invented exclusive-computer analogue; motivating chat plans do not establish an actual historical lock. See the [source falsifiers](communication-falsifiers.md) and [resource scope](resource-study-design.md).

| Resource R1: recover an unused opportunity | Before agent-0's next decision after its last executed legal wait occurred with unfinished legal independent work, provided independent work is still unfinished. Active note asks it to distinguish which task needs the computer; neutral note uses the same boundary. The previous wait is observed execution, not a proposed next action. | Whole-swarm fraction of eight executed task completions. A content-independent truthful task-specific-wait script should rarely trigger; a global-wait script can exercise the trigger without proving beliefs. | Resource R2: react to observed reopening | Before the first agent-0 decision where its own previous observed status was external occupation, its current observed status is available, and its computer task is unfinished. Agreement or source deduplication without accuracy improvement does not support a correctness benefit. A future unique-source or generic-caution comparison could challenge the specific account, but it needs new supported world parameters and registration. | A relay of the same report ID alone is not two distinct reports. The event may arrive after a final verdict or on the last slot. Three rounds may allow a verdict change but no further dissemination. Source IDs imply shared measurements only by this generator's rules. |
| Communication C2: late canonical-fragment gap | In the complementary modular-sum world, immediately before agent-0's second own decision when no submission exists and fewer than four canonical originals are in its scoped inventory. Active note asks it to identify and request/relay missing canonical components through legal routes; neutral note shares the boundary. | Mean four-agent exact modular-total accuracy, missing submissions zero. A disconnected graph can falsify any claim that the reminder overcomes impossible routes. | An attachment gap is not measured ignorance: free text can convey values. With three rounds, only the second communication turn and final submission remain. Built-in multicast worlds permit two-round all-gather, but bandwidth/fanout differs by topology. A late note cannot prevent a premature first-turn answer. |

Timing should not force the desired outcome. Scripts that ignore context must produce the same behavior under active and neutral notes for a fixed world; deliberately adverse scripts should also remain legal. Trace checks should show no note before eligibility, exactly one receipt at the pinned boundary, unchanged future-information scope, no hidden action retries and intact nondelivery. These are infrastructure checks, not empirical LLM results.

## Call bounds, inference resolution and stopping

For a four-agent world with `R` allocated turns per agent, two content arms and `n` runs per arm allocate at most `2 × n × 4 × R` subject calls. Pure tool-state triggers require no additional model calls. A model detector, candidate/revision stage or rollback strategy has a separate preregistered call/token budget. Early completion can reduce actual calls; stopping because an effect looks favorable is not the declared horizon.

| Proposed pilot | Assigned units | Maximum subject calls | Minimum two-sided exact-test resolution |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| One resource trigger, two content arms, two runs/arm, six rounds | 4 independent swarms | 96 | `2 / choose(4,2) = 1/3`; cannot reject at 0.05 |
| One communication trigger at a fixed topology, two runs/arm, three rounds | 4 independent networks | 48 | `1/3`; cannot reject at 0.05 |
| Two timing or topology levels × two content levels, two runs/cell, three rounds | 8 independent networks | 96 | Each conditional factor test has `choose(4,2)^2 = 36` allocations; floor `2/36 ≈ 0.0556` before multiplicity |
| The same four-cell design in a six-round resource world | 8 independent swarms | 192 | Same factor-test resolution; extra turns do not create extra trials |
| Two isolated active/neutral paired-world blocks, six rounds | 2 independent pairs, 4 executions | 96 | `2 / 2^2 = 1/2` for a two-sided paired sign-permutation test |

These are mathematical resolution floors for symmetric registered assignment tests, not power guarantees; ties can make them worse. Four runs per arm permit an unstratified theoretical floor `2/70`, while six paired blocks permit `2/64`; neither sample size promises adequate power or narrow intervals. Two primary factorial contrasts need a declared family correction. Sequential adaptive tests cannot inherit these fixed-allocation formulas without a separately justified design-aware test.

Before hosted execution, use separate deterministic fixtures to check whether the task is legally feasible within the horizon, whether triggers can occur early enough to matter, and whether the intended action opportunities remain available. Shadow evaluation on existing frozen traces may estimate descriptive trigger frequency; it cannot reveal the outcome under an inserted note or select a favorable trigger without fresh confirmation. Label prior interventions in those traces: their affected histories are not an untreated trigger distribution. Floor/ceiling task outcomes and low exposure should motivate an explicitly amended pilot, not retrospective exclusions or a claim of no effect.

Freeze timing, wording, scope, assignment probabilities, delivery cap, outcomes, sharp-null test, minimum meaningful effect, sample size, family correction and failure handling before seeing arm outcomes. Preserve failed and conflicting results. Infrastructure failures retain partial execution and block complete estimation; behavioral nondelivery, invalid actions and budget exhaustion remain in their assigned outcomes. The first feasible milestone is a well-instrumented policy comparison with uncertainty, not a discovered latent belief or a simulator certified free of confounds.
