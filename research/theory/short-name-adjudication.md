It is not a calibrated label set, an estimate of mention precision, representative behavioral prevalence, or evidence of delivery, reading, influence, or a new behavioral discovery. No graph, source, saved report, database, or classifier was changed. No database was opened and no external model/API call was made for this review.

## Deterministic selection

Before assigning review descriptions, I selected every o1 candidate and sixteen chronological o3 quantiles. For each target, candidates retain the function's ordering by `(timestamp, message ID)`. The o3 zero-based ranks are `floor(i * (77 - 1) / 15)` for `i = 0..15`: `0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 76`. This yields 20 distinct messages and 20 candidate pairs. It deliberately gives all four rare o1 candidates coverage, so the combined sample is not a probability sample of the 81 candidates. Chronological quantiles also do not supply inclusion probabilities or independence for prevalence estimation.

The descriptions below are provisional readings of complete message text. Quoted clauses are exact original substrings, and line numbers refer to decompressed `ai-village/chat_messages.jsonl.gz`, not chronological ranks. ## Consequences for network interpretation

Rejecting third-person candidates from an *address* projection could substantially change incoming weighted degree and its concentration: the unfiltered candidate expansion gives o3 77 incoming name-reference pairs and o1 four, while the minimum-three-character baseline admits neither name. These are lexical counts, not observed address counts. I do not extrapolate the review descriptions to all 81 candidates or recompute centrality from a partial adjudication. Degree, reciprocity, path structure, and spectral quantities would need recalculation on a complete, versioned projection before any magnitude or ranking claim.

The o1 cases show why the meaning of an edge matters. An agent can acquire incoming references as an absent model, a historical example, or the subject of a human correction. That is compatible with **topic prominence** and incompatible with inferring that it relayed current tasks merely because its name has incoming edges. The o3 cases mix introductions, task ownership, repeated status reports, proposal attribution, and actual address language. Popularity of an artifact owner or repetition of recap text can produce a hub in an entity-reference projection without establishing brokerage over a delivery network. Opposite-direction mentions likewise do not prove reciprocal exchange or mutual reading.

A defensible future validation would freeze the target construct first. For an address projection, adjudicate every candidate or a justified probability sample using whole messages and bounded context, retain disagreement/unknown cases, and separate apparent address from reported contact and actual tool delivery evidence. For an entity-reference projection, third-person matches can be valid observations, but the edges must retain that interpretation. These projections can coexist without collapsing attribution, ownership, addressing, delivery, and influence into one relation. A registry should preserve original text spans, instrument/alias policy, and exclusions; normalized spans cannot be back-projected into original text without an explicit map.

This review is a measurement falsifier, not a causal study. No change in name eligibility can by itself reveal communication opportunities, network interference, psychological traits, or a novel social mechanism. A future experiment would need an explicit delivery graph and scheduler, complete exposure records, and whole-network or otherwise interference-aware units; the historical candidate graph cannot supply those conditions by assumption.
