# Optional analogue: exclusive computer and independent work

This world tests a narrow question: does a private reminder to distinguish task requirements change executed completion when a shared computer is sometimes unavailable and independent work remains legal? It is one additional capability, not a model of all waiting or an ontology of agent society. Historical mechanism remains unestablished.

The initial design uses four agents, each with one independent task and one computer-gated task. Both tasks and their action costs are visible to their owner. The computer is initially occupied by an external user and becomes available at an exogenously sampled round. Subjects see its current state, but not its future release round or generator seed. A successful request grants one exclusive lease; gated work requires that lease and automatically releases it when the task completes. Voluntary release, recipient-scoped peer messages, independent work, and waiting are legal actions. Independent work remains legal while the computer is closed, held by another agent, or held by the acting agent. Messaging does not grant access or complete work.

The [source falsifier audit](communication-falsifiers.md) includes a reported plan to prepare a response while waiting for computer availability (message `55574280-065c-4390-b66e-f0dba7c028ce`, April 22, 2025), and reports of work proceeding despite a blocked asset or peer publishing task. The audit also finds multiple agents reporting simultaneous computer sessions. These are reports and plans, not a verified exclusive-computer lock or executed preparation oracle. They motivate keeping independent work legal and rebut a universal global-wait account. They do not justify treating this invented exclusive lease as an observed historical constraint.

Alternatives considered include a resource queue, public reservations, uncertain reports of availability, fixed access slots, and multiple workspaces or computers. A lease is the smallest executable starting point. It preserves contention and the opportunity to do independent work without inventing a queue discipline or treating sparse chat as an access oracle. Queueing and uncertain availability would be separately declared mechanisms in later worlds. The current state is truthfully observable; this deliberately removes historical uncertainty about whether a computer was actually available.

The code oracle scores executed tasks, resource grants, denied requests, and action timing. A statement that work is complete does not change task state. A wait while an independent task remains unfinished is an observed unused opportunity; it is not evidence of a mistaken belief or latent intent. A wait after independent work is complete and computer access is blocked can be described as task-specific waiting in this generator. Scripted examples will exercise both patterns without using them as evidence about LLMs or historical agents.

The experiment randomizes the entire four-agent run to a task-specific reminder or a neutral note of matched word length, inserted privately for fixed agent-0 immediately before its first decision. Every assigned run is retained. Independent state and resource/scheduler seeds reset between runs. The primary outcome is the fraction of eight tasks completed, with unfinished tasks counted as zero; task-specific completions and waiting/access counts are descriptive. Within-run interference is permitted, so agents and turns are not independent samples. With two runs per arm, the smallest attainable two-sided exact p-value is 2/6: this is an infrastructure and behavioral pilot with wide uncertainty.

Both arms already receive the same truthful rules, including the legal independent-work action. The reminder adds no privileged access fact or future release information. An effect of its phrasing could involve several processes; a result would not identify an information-correction or attentional mechanism. The neutral comparison also does not estimate an effect relative to receiving no note.

This comparison identifies the effect of the declared note in the declared task/resource/scheduler bundle under independence and backend-stability assumptions. It does not isolate a cognitive mechanism, mediation by waiting, general strategic patience, optimal scheduling, or historical communication effects. A neutral note need not be inert, matched words do not guarantee matched tokens, and fixed action costs differ from real clock time or computational effort. Different realized lease opportunities and actor order are part of the generator; their imbalance is reported rather than used to exclude runs. Positive, negative, and null results are all valid. Source observations and falsifiers will be linked as motivation separately from these invented tasks, costs, and release dynamics.

## Implemented API

`swarm_lab.resource_environment` exposes `create_resource_spec`, `validate_resource_spec`, `create_resource_environment`, `resource_environment_capabilities`, `check_resource_contract`, and `resource_subject_request`. The constructed kind is `exclusive_resource_tasks`. It implements reset, scoped observation, step, private context insertion, privileged snapshot, and code evaluation. Defaults are four agents, six allocated rounds, release-round choices `[1, 2]`, and one decision step per task; computer work also requires a preceding successful acquisition. Independent work is interleaved within shared rounds rather than executing real concurrent processes. A release equal to the maximum round models a computer that never becomes available within the horizon. A release of zero models initial availability. General browser work, repeated occupation windows, arbitrary populations and queues are unsupported.

`swarm_lab.resource_experiments` exposes `create_resource_protocol(trials_per_cell=2, seed=137, ...)`, `validate_resource_protocol`, `randomize_resource_runs`, `run_resource_experiment`, `analyze_resource_runs`, `replay_resource_report`, and `resource_code_hashes`. The two fixed arms are `neutral` and `task_reminder`, with 36-word default notes. Two runs per arm and six rounds allocate at most 96 subject calls. Only the supplied runner can make model calls; the default is a context-independent scripted policy. There are no database writes in these modules.

An output directory receives the frozen protocol, balanced assignments, selected source archive and each privileged initial state before subject calls. Requests contain no run/arm metadata, future release time, seed or global task-progress state. Private context is inserted for agent-0 at its first scheduled boundary and retained only within that run. Completed and failed actions remain in traces. Unknown execution failures preserve the pending request and partial state, abort analysis, and raise `ResourceExecutionError`; there is no automatic replay or replacement. Reusing a directory that already contains a result is blocked. Hosted Responses execution requires a registered source hash for the frozen subject adapter and pre-call artifacts. Backend identity and absence of cross-run subject memory still rely on the caller contract.

The analysis uses a two-sided whole-swarm randomization test for the sharp no-effect null and a conservative bounded independent-run interval, plus a run bootstrap sensitivity interval. This is one registered primary contrast; other metrics are descriptive. Replay makes zero model calls, reconstructs every scoped request/action/result and code-oracle outcome, and recomputes the numerical analysis. Replay verifies the recorded synthetic execution, not the provenance of a supplied callback or a historical mechanism.

Validation includes context-independent null scripts, deliberately adverse and favorable callback fixtures, truthful task-specific waits, global-wait scripts, exact reset, exclusive lease permissions, private information scope, feasible complete execution, invalid-action retention, balanced independent seeds, source/backend mismatch, pre-call artifacts, failure retention and tamper rejection. These tests are infrastructure evidence only. Existing scientific modules and the current authoring inventory remain unchanged until the host explicitly integrates this additional capability.

## Quantitative claim boundaries

The estimand is the difference in whole-swarm mean executed completion under a private note for fixed agent-0, including any downstream interference within that swarm. It is not a per-agent direct effect, an effect relative to no note, or a historical waiting mechanism. Eight tasks per run are outcomes; they do not multiply the randomized sample size. A difference of 0.125 is 12.5 percentage points, not a relative percentage increase. The sum of run-level completion fractions is also distinct from the actual number of completed tasks.

At two independent runs per arm there are six balanced allocations and the smallest attainable two-sided exact p-value is one third; ties can raise it further. This design cannot reject at 0.05. A zero sample difference does not establish equivalence. All sampled runs can sit at a completion floor or ceiling, collapsing the run-bootstrap sensitivity interval while the conservative independent-run interval remains wide. Longer horizons or easier tasks can mechanically create ceilings; insufficient budgets or delayed release can create floors. Those design properties must remain visible rather than be treated as absent behavioral effects.

Wait and request counts describe executed process. A wait with unfinished independent work is an unused legal opportunity, not a measured belief. A denied request is any valid request without a new grant, including requests while already holding the lease or after one's computer task is finished; it is not necessarily evidence of blocked access. Fewer waits or more grants are post-treatment outcomes and do not identify mediation, mental-state correction, work quality, optimal scheduling or real elapsed cost.

`swarm_lab.resource_claims.build_resource_fact_ledger` constructs a finite registry from an exact resource report, with optional pinned registered object `{id, version, hash}`. It performs zero-call scoped action replay, checks frozen source hashes and backend/receipt declarations, and recomputes the numerical analysis. A complete-but-inconsistent or incomplete report exposes status facts but no usable outcome/effect facts. Canonical report and registered object hashes are separate identities and both are checked when a source pin is supplied. The registry does not attest action provider origin or archive bytes; the separate host execution audit handles archived source bytes. Generic finite claim schemas and checks can validate explicit values against this registry, but attached prose, cognitive mechanisms, historical access claims and generalization remain unverified.
